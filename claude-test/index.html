Why Payroll Giving?

Give the same. More reaches charity.

Payroll Giving takes a donation from gross salary, before income tax. The tax that would otherwise go to HMRC can reach the charity instead.

How it works

Four steps, then it runs with payroll.

  1. 1

    An employee chooses

    They pick a charity recognised by HMRC and an amount. They can change or stop it at any time.

  2. 2

    Payroll deducts

    The gift comes out of gross pay in the normal pay run, after National Insurance and before income tax.

  3. 3

    The agency distributes

    The employer sends one payment to the Payroll Giving agency, which pays each charity.

  4. 4

    The charity is paid

    Money arrives as regular income, usually unrestricted, with no Gift Aid claim to process.

Questions

The things employers ask first.

How long does set-up take?

Most employers can be ready within one payroll cycle. The longer part is usually telling people the scheme exists and answering their questions.

Does it change National Insurance?

No. Donations are taken after National Insurance is worked out and before income tax, so the relief applies to income tax only.

What is the fee?

We are planning a 3% Payroll Giving Agency fee. Employers can choose to cover it so the full donation reaches the charity. Final fees will be confirmed before launch.

Which charities can employees choose?

Any charity recognised by HMRC for tax purposes. Employees choose for themselves, so an employer does not have to pick a single charity for everyone.

Is Payroll Giving always the better option?

No. It suits people paid through PAYE who pay income tax. If someone pays no income tax there is no relief to apply, and giving post-tax with Gift Aid may suit them better.

What happens to my donation if you are not approved?

The £5,000 stays ring-fenced while our registration is considered. If we do not secure charitable status and the approvals needed to operate as a Payroll Giving Agency, donations are returned.
